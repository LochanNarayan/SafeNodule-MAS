# Lung Nodule Pathology Reference (demo knowledge base)

## Invasive adenocarcinoma
Invasive adenocarcinoma often presents as a part-solid or solid nodule with
irregular or spiculated margins. Spiculation reflects desmoplastic reaction and
tumor extension into surrounding parenchyma. Pleural indentation and vascular
convergence signs are associated with invasive growth. Larger size (>10 mm) and
interval growth on serial imaging increase the probability of invasion.

## Minimally invasive adenocarcinoma
Minimally invasive adenocarcinoma typically appears as a part-solid nodule with
a small solid component (<5 mm invasive focus). Margins may be lobulated. It
carries a better prognosis than invasive adenocarcinoma.

## Pre-invasive lesions (AAH / AIS)
Atypical adenomatous hyperplasia and adenocarcinoma in situ usually appear as
pure ground-glass nodules, often small (<6-10 mm), with smooth margins and no
solid component, spiculation, or pleural retraction.

## Fleischner Society guidance
Part-solid nodules carry the highest malignancy risk of the density categories.
Solid nodules >8 mm warrant further workup (PET-CT, biopsy, or short-interval
follow-up). Pure ground-glass nodules are followed conservatively.

## Lung-RADS (screening)
- Category 2: benign appearance, solid <6 mm or GGO <30 mm.
- Category 3: probably benign, solid 6-8 mm.
- Category 4A/4B/4X: suspicious; solid >8 mm, part-solid with growing solid
  component, or spiculated margins; recommend PET-CT / tissue sampling.
